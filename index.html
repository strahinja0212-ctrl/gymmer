Phase 1 rules

  • Weight choice: pick a weight where the last rep feels like you could still do 2–3 more. Write it down (Tracker tab).
  • Progression: when you hit the top of the rep range on all sets with clean form, add the smallest increment (2–2.5 kg, machines: one plate).
  • Soreness is normal the first 2 weeks; sharp joint pain is not — stop and check the form video.
  • Off days: walk. Aim for 8–10k steps daily.

Phase III — Grow Months 4–6 · 4 days/week

Rules

Same split. Now it's progressive overload, every week.

  • Double progression: when you hit the top of the rep range on all sets → next session add +2.5 kg (upper body) / +5 kg (lower body). If you added weight and fell below the range, stay there and build reps back up.
  • Effort: last set of each exercise ends 1–2 reps shy of failure. Isolation moves (raises, curls) can go to failure occasionally.
  • Deload: every 6–8 weeks, or when everything feels heavy and sleep/motivation dips — one week at ~60% of your usual weights, same movements. You come back stronger.
  • Optional 5th day: 30–40 min easy cardio (zone 2 — you can hold a conversation) or a long walk. Helps recovery and appetite, doesn't steal gains.
  • Machine busy? Swap like-for-like: leg press ↔ hack squat · lat pulldown ↔ assisted pull-up · cable row ↔ chest-supported DB row · DB press ↔ machine press.

What to expect (checkpoints in the Tracker tab)

  • Weeks 1–3: better energy and mood; weights climb fast (that's your nervous system learning).
  • Weeks 4–8: clothes fit differently; every lift is up noticeably.
  • Weeks 8–12: visible change in the mirror. This is where most people who quit never get.
  • Sleep 7–9 h. It's not optional; it's where the muscle is actually built.

Field manual

Your first day, step by step

  • Bring: a bottle of water, a small towel (wipe the bench after you), and this page open on your phone.
  • Arrive 10 min early the first time and just walk the floor once. Find the squat rack, the cable tower, the leg press. Knowing where things are removes the reason most first sessions get abandoned.
  • Finding your weight: do one set of 10 with something clearly too light. Add a little. Repeat until a set of 8 leaves you with 2–3 reps left in you. That number is your starting weight — write it in the Tracker tab. Session 1 is meant to feel too easy.
  • Equipment busy? Ask "how many sets do you have left?" or "can I work in?". Both are completely normal; nobody minds.
  • Nobody is watching you. Everyone in there is looking at their own phone between sets, exactly like you're about to.
  • If a session goes badly — bad sleep, no energy, a lift feels wrong — do the first three exercises and leave. A short session still counts on the calendar.

When life gets in the way

  • Missed one session: never miss twice in a row. Do it the next day and carry on; don't try to cram two sessions together.
  • No time today: do the first three exercises and leave. Those are the ones that matter, and 25 minutes keeps the streak alive. A short session beats a skipped one every single time.
  • Missed a whole week: nothing is lost — literally nothing, at one week. Pick up exactly where you left off, same weights.
  • Two to four weeks off: restart at ~90% of your old weights and rebuild over two weeks. It comes back far faster than it was built.
  • Over a month off: go back to the previous phase for two weeks — Phase 2 if you were in Phase 3 — then resume. Your form is rustier than your strength.
  • Sick: symptoms above the neck (sniffles) → train lighter. Below the neck (chest, fever, aching) → rest until a day after it clears, then restart at 90%.
  • Travelling: hotel or no gym → push-ups, split squats, hip thrusts and planks, 3 rounds, 20 min. It holds what you have.
  • Deload weeks: every 6–8 weeks, or whenever everything feels heavy and motivation dips — one week of the same sessions at ~60% of your weights. Planned easy weeks are how people train for years without stopping.

Stop and check

  • Sharp or stabbing joint pain — stop that exercise for the session. Re-watch the form video, drop the weight by half next time. Pain that persists a week: see a doctor or physio.
  • Dizzy, light-headed, or seeing spots — sit down, drink water. Usually under-eating or holding your breath through reps.
  • A lift going backwards two sessions running — that is a recovery problem, not a training one. Check sleep, calories and stress before you change the programme.
  • Soreness that lasts past 72 h — you added too much too fast. Repeat last week's weights rather than pushing again.
  • Nothing here is a reason to quit for good — every one of them is a reason to train a bit lighter next session.

Where the calories come from

Protein target is the science-backed 1.6–2.2 g/kg (Morton 2018 meta-analysis; ISSN position stand). Hit protein and calories daily; the carb/fat split is flexible.

What the macros do

Protein 150 g/day▶
What it does for you
Every hard set puts microscopic damage into your muscle fibres. Protein is the raw material your body rebuilds them with — slightly thicker than before, if you gave it enough. That rebuilding is the entire point of lifting; without the material, the stimulus is wasted. Protein is also the most filling of the three macros and the one your body is worst at storing as fat.
Your target
150 g/day — about 2.1 g per kg of bodyweight, comfortably inside the 1.6–2.2 g/kg range the research supports. Split it roughly evenly: ~35–40 g at each of four meals beats 150 g in one sitting. For scale, 40 g is 170 g of chicken breast, or six eggs, or one scoop of whey plus a pot of Greek yoghurt.
If you fall short
You train, you get sore, and you grow slowly or not at all. Below roughly 1.6 g/kg the muscle you gain from the same training measurably drops off. This is the single most common beginner mistake — training like an athlete and eating like someone who isn't.
Carbohydrates ~440 g/day▶
What it does for you
Glycogen is what a heavy set actually runs on. Muscles topped up with it are stronger, recover between sets faster, and look visibly fuller. Carbs also protect your protein: with enough of them around, the protein you eat goes to rebuilding muscle instead of being burnt for energy. And they pull water into muscle tissue, which is part of why the first weeks of eating properly make you look bigger quickly.
Your target
~440 g/day, which is whatever is left after protein and fat are set. Concentrate the biggest portions around training — a solid meal 1.5–2 hours before, and carbs with protein in the couple of hours after. Daily total still matters far more than timing.
If you fall short
Sessions go flat by the third exercise, your working weights stall, and you stay sore longer. Low-carb dieting and hard lifting is a fight you have no reason to pick while you're trying to gain.
Fat ~80 g/day▶
What it does for you
Fat runs your hormones, testosterone included, and testosterone is not a detail when you're trying to add muscle. It builds the membrane of every cell you own, and it's the vehicle that carries vitamins A, D, E and K into you — eat those vitamins without fat and much of them passes straight through. It also makes food taste like food, which matters more than it sounds over a six-month bulk.
Your target
~80 g/day, about 1.1 g per kg. Olive oil on the salad, whole eggs, nuts, kikiriki puter, oily fish, full-fat dairy. You do not need to chase this number — hit protein, hit calories, and fat lands here on its own.
If you fall short
Held much under 0.8 g/kg for months, testosterone drifts down, joints get creaky, and the fat-soluble vitamins stop being absorbed properly. Very low fat is not a virtue; it's just a slower way to feel worse.
Fiber 25–30 g/day▶
What it does for you
It keeps digestion regular, which stops being a theoretical benefit and becomes a very practical one the moment you start eating 3,100 kcal a day. It slows the release of everything eaten alongside it, so energy is steadier. And the foods that carry it — vegetables, beans, whole grains, fruit — are the same foods carrying most of your vitamins and minerals, so hitting fiber quietly fixes your micronutrients too.
Your target
25–30 g/day. Pasulj or sočivo a couple of times a week, oats at breakfast, whole bread instead of white, and vegetables at two meals covers it without any special effort. Drink water alongside it or it works against you.
If you fall short
Bloating and constipation on a high-calorie diet — miserable, and enough on its own to make people quit a bulk. It also means you're skipping vegetables, which is the actual problem.
Calories ~3,100/day▶
What it does for you
The total sets the direction: eat more than you burn and you gain weight, less and you lose it. Your macros decide what that gained weight is made of. Both have to be right at once — a surplus without enough protein mostly makes fat, and all the protein in the world without a surplus makes very slow progress.
Your target
~3,100/day — roughly 2,700 kcal maintenance for a 70 kg, ~185 cm trainee plus a deliberate 400 kcal surplus. That's aimed at 0.25–0.5 kg gained per week, about as fast as a new lifter can add mostly muscle. Weigh yourself 2–3 mornings a week and compare weekly averages, not single days.
If you fall short
Undershoot and you stay exactly the same size no matter how well you train — the most demoralising way to waste six months. Overshoot badly and you do gain, but an increasing share is fat you'll have to diet back off later. No gain in two weeks → add 300 kcal. Gaining faster than ~0.5 kg a week → take 200 back.

The 6 rules (everything else is detail)

  • Protein at every meal — 4 meals × ~35–40 g beats one giant dinner.
  • Drink ~3 L of water a day — more on training days. Muscle is roughly 75% water, creatine pulls more of it in, and mild dehydration costs you strength and makes 3,100 kcal of food sit badly. Pale-yellow urine is the whole test.
  • Steady surplus — you should gain 0.25–0.5 kg per week. Weigh yourself 2–3× a week, mornings, and compare weekly averages.
  • Adjust by the scale, not by feel — no gain for 2 weeks → add 300 kcal (a glass of milk + a spoon of kikiriki puter). Gaining faster than ~0.5 kg a week → cut 200 kcal.
  • Don't drink calories — juices and sodas add fat, not muscle. Water, coffee, tea.
  • Most carbs around training — a solid meal 1.5–2 h before, protein + carbs within a couple of hours after. Daily totals still matter most.

When you can't eat that much

  • Drink the gap. Milk, whey, a blended banana + oats + kikiriki puter — liquid calories don't fill you up the way a plate does. Half a litre of milk is 275 kcal you barely notice.
  • Add fat, not volume. A spoon of olive oil on the rice, a spoon of kikiriki puter in the yoghurt — ~100 kcal each with no extra bulk in your stomach.
  • Eat before you're hungry. Fixed meal times beat appetite on a bulk. If you wait until you want food, you'll finish the day 500 kcal short.
  • Cook once, eat three times. The days you fall short are the days there was nothing ready. Roast 1.5 kg of chicken and a tray of potatoes on Sunday.
  • Keep an emergency 500 kcal in the cupboard — oats, kikiriki puter, milk powder. Faster than deciding what to do about it.
  • Don't go the other way. If you're gaining faster than ~0.5 kg a week, that extra is fat you'll diet off later. Steady is the point.

A day of eating ~3,200 kcal · ~170 g P

Two batch cooks feed the whole day: burritos every ~10 days, lunch boxes every week.

Breakfast ~900 kcal · 53 g P

1 chicken burrito + a glass of milk

Fridge: microwave 1.5–2 min in a damp paper towel. Frozen: move it to the fridge the night before.

Lunch ~850 kcal · 50 g P

Teriyaki chicken box: chicken, rice, broccoli

Microwave 2–3 min with a splash of water on the rice. Lazy day: tuna pasta (125 g pasta + 1 can tuna + passata + cheese, 12 min).

Snack ~700 kcal · 25 g P

80 g oats + 300 ml milk + 1 banana + 1 spoon kikiriki puter

Stir in the daily 5 g creatine here.

Dinner ~750 kcal · 45 g P

1 chicken burrito

Still hungry: a second glass of milk or a yoghurt.

Batch 1 — 20 burritos every ~10 days · ~2 h

  • Rice: cook 500 g, let it cool.
  • Chicken: 3 kg thighs in small cubes, fried in 2–3 batches with taco seasoning (or paprika + cumin + garlic + salt).
  • Veg: fry 4 peppers + 3 onions, ~5 min.
  • Mix: chicken, rice, veg, 3 cans beans, 2 cans corn (both drained) in one big pot.
  • Roll: warm each tortilla 10 s, filling + a handful of cheese (800 g total), fold the sides, roll tight.
  • Store: wrap each in foil. 4–6 in the fridge (3–4 days max), the rest frozen (3 months).

Keep sour cream and salsa out of the filling, they go watery frozen. Add them fresh.

Batch 2 — 7 lunch boxes weekly · ~40 min

  • Chicken: 1.5 kg thighs in cubes, fried. Add 4 spoons soy sauce + 2 spoons honey + 2 cloves garlic, reduce 2 min.
  • Rice: cook 700 g.
  • Box: chicken + rice + a handful of frozen broccoli or mixed veg, into 7 boxes.
  • Store: 3 in the fridge, 4 frozen.

Cook it alongside the burrito chicken on burrito day and it costs almost no extra time.
